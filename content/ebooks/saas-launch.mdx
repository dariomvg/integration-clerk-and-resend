# 30 Days to Launch Your First SaaS

## Introduction

Launching a SaaS is no longer reserved for large teams with months of development time. Modern frameworks, AI-assisted coding, and cloud platforms have dramatically reduced the barriers to building software products.

This book provides a practical 30-day roadmap designed to help you move from an idea to a public launch with a focused, minimal product.

The goal isn't perfection—it's momentum.

---

# Week 1: Find a Problem Worth Solving

## Day 1: Start with Pain

Don't begin with features.

Begin with problems people already complain about.

Look for recurring frustrations in:

- Reddit communities
- X (Twitter) discussions
- Discord servers
- Customer reviews
- Your own workflow

A strong SaaS usually removes friction rather than creating excitement.

## Day 2: Define One Customer

Avoid building for everyone.

Create a simple profile:

- Job
- Daily tasks
- Biggest frustration
- Budget
- Existing alternatives

Specific audiences make marketing easier.

## Day 3: Validate Quickly

Talk to potential users.

Ask questions like:

- How do you solve this today?
- What takes the most time?
- Would you pay to make this easier?

Listen more than you speak.

## Day 4: Study Competitors

Competition is evidence that demand exists.

Analyze:

- Pricing
- Positioning
- Missing features
- User complaints

Your advantage often comes from focus.

## Day 5–7: Define the MVP

Your MVP should solve one important problem.

Avoid adding:

- Teams
- Notifications
- Complex dashboards
- Advanced analytics

Keep only what helps users reach their goal.

---

# Week 2: Build the Foundation

## Day 8: Choose Your Stack

A modern stack might include:

- Next.js
- TypeScript
- PostgreSQL
- Prisma
- Tailwind CSS

Keep the technology familiar.

## Day 9: Authentication

Implement secure authentication early.

Support:

- Sign in
- Sign up
- Password reset
- Session management

Don't reinvent security.

## Day 10: Database Design

Start simple.

Ask:

- What are the main entities?
- How do they relate?
- What data will grow?

Good models reduce future complexity.

## Day 11–12: Core Feature

Build the feature users actually came for.

Ignore everything else until this works.

## Day 13: Payments

Connect a payment provider.

Include:

- Monthly subscription
- Success page
- Cancellation flow
- Billing management

Test every scenario.

## Day 14: Internal Testing

Use your product like a customer.

Write down every friction point.

---

# Week 3: Polish and Prepare

## Day 15–16: Improve UX

Small improvements matter.

Focus on:

- Empty states
- Loading states
- Error messages
- Navigation

Good UX removes confusion.

## Day 17: Add Analytics

Measure behavior.

Track:

- Signups
- Conversions
- Active users
- Retention

Data guides future decisions.

## Day 18: Documentation

Create:

- README
- Help page
- FAQ
- Contact page

Reduce support before it happens.

## Day 19: Landing Page

Your landing page should answer:

1. What is it?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why is it better?

Keep messaging simple.

## Day 20–21: Beta Users

Invite a small group.

Watch them use the product.

Their actions reveal more than their opinions.

---

# Week 4: Launch

## Day 22–24: Fix Critical Issues

Prioritize problems that block users.

Ignore cosmetic improvements.

## Day 25: Create Launch Assets

Prepare:

- Screenshots
- Demo video
- Social posts
- Product description

Consistency builds trust.

## Day 26: Soft Launch

Share with:

- Friends
- Communities
- Early subscribers

Collect feedback before wider exposure.

## Day 27: Public Launch

Launch on platforms like:

- Product Hunt
- X
- LinkedIn
- Relevant communities

Stay available for questions.

## Day 28–30: Learn

Review:

- Traffic
- Signups
- Payments
- Feedback

Every launch produces valuable data.

# Pricing Basics

Avoid overthinking pricing.

A simple structure often works best.

| Plan | Price |
|------|--------|
| Starter | $9/month |
| Pro | $29/month |
| Team | $99/month |

Price can change later.

Shipping cannot happen later.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Building too many features
- Waiting for perfection
- Ignoring customer conversations
- Launching without analytics
- Changing direction every week

Consistency beats constant reinvention.

---

# Final Thoughts

Launching your first SaaS isn't about creating the next billion-dollar company.

It's about learning how to identify problems, build solutions, talk to customers, and iterate based on real feedback.

The first launch creates experience.

The second launch builds confidence.

The third launch often builds momentum.
